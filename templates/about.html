When does disorder stabilize an alloy?

In ordered FeCo, iron and cobalt occupy different sublattices. A random solid solution lets either element occupy either sublattice. The extra arrangements give the solid solution an entropy advantage that can outweigh the energetic preference for ordering.

Developed by Wei Chen · UCLouvain

Heating equiatomic FeCo

At 0 K the disordered body-centered cubic (BCC) solution costs 53.0 meV/atom more than ordered B2. Its ideal configurational entropy is kB ln 2 per atom. Each 100 K of heating reduces the free-energy difference by 5.97 meV/atom. The model predicts a crossover near 888 K.

Free energy of a random solution

For each lattice α (BCC, FCC or HCP), the model adds elemental reference energies and pairwise mixing enthalpies. The pair interactions Ω come from density-functional calculations on 16-atom binary special quasirandom structures. All energies here are per atom.

At fixed composition the ideal configurational entropy is constant. Multiplication by temperature gives the increasing free-energy benefit of disorder.

Competition with other phases

The lowest-energy random lattice competes with pure elements, equimolar solutions of elemental subsets, and binary and ternary intermetallics from AFLOW’s ICSD and LIB collections. The convex hull gives the lowest free energy of any combination of candidates at the requested composition.

ΔGhull=minα{{ g() }}−Ghull

A positive gap favors decomposition. For a stable solution the gap is zero, and the app reports its inverse hull energy relative to competing stable phases.

The original study screened 658,008 equimolar five-element alloys. This app also accepts unequal fractions and compositions with 2–8 supported elements. Intermetallic energies are held constant with temperature. Cooling rate and diffusion can affect which phases form in a sample.
