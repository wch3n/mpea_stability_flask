What stabilizes a random alloy?

Many elements can share a crystal lattice in countless arrangements. That disorder lowers the free energy at high temperature. A competing ordered compound can still lie lower.

Why equiatomic AlCo orders

The lowest-energy disordered AlCo solution is hexagonal close-packed (HCP). At the same composition, the ordered B2 compound has a lower free energy throughout the modeled temperature range.

How a prediction is made

Set the temperature

The formula gives the atomic fraction xi of each element. The app estimates the alloy melting point by weighting elemental melting points by those fractions, then multiplies by the temperature factor you enter.

T = f ∑i xi Tm,i

Compare three lattices

For each body-centered cubic, face-centered cubic and hexagonal close-packed lattice, the model adds elemental reference energies, pairwise mixing enthalpies and the ideal configurational entropy term. Each pair interaction Ω comes from density-functional calculations on a 16-atom binary special quasirandom structure.

G = ∑i xiEi + ∑i<j Ωijxixj + kBT ∑i xi ln xi

Test for decomposition

The convex hull gives the lowest free energy attainable by combining candidate phases at the requested composition. Candidates include the pure elements, equimolar solutions of their subsets, the requested solution, and binary and ternary intermetallics from AFLOW’s ICSD and LIB collections.

A solution on the hull is stable within this set. Its inverse hull energy measures its depth relative to competing phases. For an unstable solution, a positive gap gives the energy released per atom on decomposition.

The original study screened 658,008 equimolar five-element alloys. This app also accepts unequal fractions and compositions with 2–8 supported elements. The calculation assumes random occupation in solid solutions and treats intermetallic energies as temperature independent. It estimates equilibrium stability. Cooling rate and diffusion can affect which phases form in a sample.
